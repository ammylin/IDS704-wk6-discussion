    📖 Case Study Connection: The Gender Shades Study

    The landmark Gender Shades study (Joy Buolamwini & Timnit Gebru, 2018) evaluated commercial facial classification systems from major tech vendors. The study demonstrated that while overall vendor error rates were low (under 1–5% overall error rate), disaggregating performance revealed stark intersectional disparities: error rates for lighter-skinned males were under 1%, whereas error rates for darker-skinned females reached up to 34.7%.

    Note on Fictional Scenario: Campus FacePass and all numerical figures in this activity are fictional scenario data created for classroom discussion. The case study is inspired by real-world algorithmic fairness research showing how high aggregate accuracy hides subgroup harms.

    Research Citation & Link:
    Buolamwini, J., & Gebru, T. (2018). Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research, 81, 77-91.

    TEACHING GUIDE

    📘 Facilitator Guide: Classroom Facilitation

    ⏱️ Activity Timing Comparison

    Full Discussion Mode (25–35 Mins total):

    • Intro & Role Setup: 3–5 mins
    • 4 Investigation Rounds & Events: 12–15 mins
    • Final Decisions & Evidence Citing: 5–8 mins
    • Trail Review, Class Comparison & Debrief: 5–7 mins

    Quick Mode (10–15 Mins total):

    • Intro: 2 mins | 2 Investigation Rounds + 1 Event: 5–7 mins | Final Decisions & Debrief: 3–5 mins

    🗣️ Classroom Facilitation Prompts

    • Opening the Activity: "Today we are evaluating Campus FacePass. The vendor claims 98% overall accuracy. As decision-makers, your job is not to find a single 'correct' answer, but to investigate evidence, define safeguards, and justify your choices."
    • Assigning Cards to Groups: "To see how information shapes decisions, Team 1 should start with Overall Accuracy, Team 2 with Intersectional Audit, and Team 3 with Stakeholder Feedback."
    • Handling Disagreement: "If your group is split on launching or pausing police use, identify what specific safeguard or evidence threshold would resolve the debate."
