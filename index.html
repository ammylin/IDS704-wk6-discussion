💡 Gender Shades & Real-World Case Study Connections

  • Overall Accuracy Illusion: A model can be 98% accurate overall while failing catastrophically on specific subgroups (e.g., darker-skinned females).
  • Limits of Binary & Skin Tone Partitions: Binary gender classification ignores non-binary and trans individuals. Fitzpatrick skin-typing can erase nuances within darker skin tones.
  • High-Risk vs Low-Risk Deployments: Improving error gaps does not eliminate systemic harms like surveillance, coercion, and police misidentification.

📘 Facilitator Guide: Case Study Mapping

How the Game Surfaces Key Discussion Questions:

  • Would we have discovered the problem? Round 1 shows whether teams rely on overall accuracy or ask for deeper auditing early.
  • Which population partitions should be checked? Intersectional Audit and Expand Categories push teams to consider race, gender, skin tone, age, disability, presentation, and camera conditions.
  • Is binary gender fair? Round 2 raises the problem of male/female-only evaluation and exclusion of trans/non-binary users.
  • What is gained or lost by broad grouping? Discussion on lighter/darker skin-tone framing vs statistical power vs lost nuance.
  • If companies improve error gaps, is fairness solved? Round 4 breaking update forces teams to ask if accuracy solves surveillance, consent, or police harm.
  • Is law enforcement use fair? Campus Police use case asks whether some deployments remain unacceptable regardless of accuracy.
