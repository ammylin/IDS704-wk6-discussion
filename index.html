    📖 Case Study Connection: The Gender Shades Study

    The landmark Gender Shades study (Joy Buolamwini & Timnit Gebru, 2018) evaluated commercial facial classification systems from major tech vendors. The study demonstrated that while overall vendor error rates were low (under 1–5% overall error rate), disaggregating performance revealed stark intersectional disparities: error rates for lighter-skinned males were under 1%, whereas error rates for darker-skinned females reached up to 34.7%.

    Note on Fictional Scenario: Campus FacePass and all numerical figures in this activity are fictional scenario data created for classroom discussion. The simulation is inspired by real-world algorithmic fairness research showing how high aggregate accuracy hides subgroup harms.

    Research Citation & Link:
    Buolamwini, J., & Gebru, T. (2018). Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research, 81, 77-91.

    TEACHING GUIDE

    📘 Facilitator Guide: Classroom Facilitation

    ⏱️ Activity Timing Guide (25–35 Mins total)

    • Introduction & Role Assignment: 3–5 minutes
    • Investigation Rounds & Breaking Events: 12–15 minutes
    • Final Deployment Decisions: 5–8 minutes
    • Evidence Trail, Class Share-Out & Debrief: 5–7 minutes

    🗣️ Classroom Facilitation Prompts

    • Opening the Activity: "Today we are evaluating Campus FacePass. The vendor claims 98% overall accuracy. As decision-makers, your job is to investigate incomplete evidence using your 4 tokens, define safeguards, and defend your governance decisions."
    • Handling Token Trade-offs: "Remind teams that spending a token on one area means leaving another uninvestigated. Highlight the uninvestigated evidence at the end."
    • Class Share-Out Prompts: "Compare how different stakeholder priorities and token choices led to different policy recommendations across groups."
